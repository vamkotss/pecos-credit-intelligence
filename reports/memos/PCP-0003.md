# CREDIT MEMORANDUM
**PCP-0003: Palo Duro Services**

---

## FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE

Palo Duro Services generated revenue of $15,006,829 in FY2025, representing 6.2% growth over the prior year [02_financial_statements_comparative.pdf#p1]. Gross margin stands at 42.7% [CALCULATED METRICS], indicating reasonable pricing power in specialty food manufacturing.

EBITDA declined to $1,057,405, a 12.0% contraction from $1,201,205 in FY2024 [CALCULATED METRICS]. EBITDA margin compressed to 7.0% [CALCULATED METRICS]. Net income fell to $393,624 from $498,487 in the prior year, yielding a net margin of 2.6% [CALCULATED METRICS]. Operating expenses increased to $5,345,709 despite modest revenue growth, driving the EBITDA decline.

Operating cash flow was positive at $699,755 in FY2025 [02_financial_statements_comparative.pdf#p3]. However, capital expenditures of $591,264 and debt service obligations consumed most of this cash generation [02_financial_statements_comparative.pdf#p3].

---

## BALANCE SHEET

Total assets are $6,669,193, with property, plant and equipment representing the largest component at $3,966,523 [02_financial_statements_comparative.pdf#p2]. Current assets total $2,702,670, comprising cash of $225,102, accounts receivable of $1,397,896, and inventory of $919,301 [02_financial_statements_comparative.pdf#p2].

Cash declined 76.8% over the period, falling from $970,867 to $225,102 [CALCULATED METRICS]. This deterioration reflects cumulative operating cash flow insufficient to cover capex, debt service, and prior-year member distributions.

Current liabilities are $1,741,276, yielding a current ratio of 1.55x [CALCULATED METRICS]. Total equity stands at $2,988,098, representing 44.8% of total capitalization [02_financial_statements_comparative.pdf#p2].

Accounts receivable aging shows 4.7% of receivables over 90 days [CALCULATED METRICS]. Customer concentration is severe: the largest customer (Concho Manufacturing) represents 37.6% of receivables, and the top five customers represent 89.8% of total receivables [CALCULATED METRICS].

---

## LEVERAGE AND COVERAGE

Total interest-bearing debt is $2,370,337, comprising current portion of $430,518 and long-term debt of $1,939,819 [CALCULATED METRICS]. The existing debt schedule shows three facilities: Equipment Term Loan ($438,430 balance), Owner-Occupied CRE Mortgage ($1,106,791 balance), and Term Loan A ($825,116 balance) [04_debt_schedule.pdf#p1].

**Leverage:** Total debt to EBITDA is 2.24x [CALCULATED METRICS]. This metric is within acceptable parameters and does not require structural mitigation.

**Coverage:** Debt Service Coverage Ratio is 0.58x, calculated as (EBITDA - capex - taxes) / (interest + principal) = ($1,057,405 - $591,264 - $104,634) / ($190,941 + $430,518) [CALCULATED METRICS]. This ratio **fails the 1.25x minimum requirement** by a substantial margin. The borrower is unable to service existing debt from operating cash flow after funding capital expenditures and taxes.

Interest coverage is 5.54x (EBITDA / interest expense), which is adequate [CALCULATED METRICS].

---

## FINDINGS

1. **Cash deterioration is acute.** Cash balances fell 76.8% year-over-year to $225,102, insufficient to support operations or absorb shocks [CALCULATED METRICS]. Bank statements show an ending balance of $317,144 [08_bank_statements.pdf#p4], marginally better but still constrained.

2. **DSCR failure is disqualifying.** At 0.58x, the borrower cannot service its existing debt load from operating cash flow. A $3,000,000 additional facility would worsen this position materially, adding approximately $600,000–$750,000 in annual debt service (assuming 7.5% rate and 5-year amortization).

3. **EBITDA contraction despite revenue growth signals operational stress.** Operating expenses grew faster than revenue, and EBITDA fell 12.0% year-over-year. This trend is inconsistent with the broker's assertion that "FY2025 was a strong year" [07_broker_email_thread.pdf#p1].

4. **Customer concentration creates revenue volatility risk.** Concho Manufacturing alone represents 37.6% of receivables; the top five customers represent 89.8% [CALCULATED METRICS]. Loss of any major customer would severely impair cash generation.

5. **Capital intensity is high relative to cash generation.** Capex of $591,264 consumed 84.5% of operating cash flow, leaving minimal headroom for debt service or growth investment [02_financial_statements_comparative.pdf#p3].

6. **Stated use of proceeds includes refinancing seller notes.** The application indicates proceeds will refinance seller notes and support a distribution centre lease [01_loan_application.pdf#p1]. Refinancing existing obligations does not improve the borrower's debt service capacity; it merely extends maturity.

7. **Financial statements are reviewed but not audited.** Statements are prepared internally and reviewed annually by an independent accounting firm but not audited [06_borrower_questionnaire.pdf#p1], limiting assurance on accuracy.

---

## RECOMMENDATION

**DECLINE**

The borrower fails the primary coverage requirement. A DSCR of 0.58x indicates the company cannot service its existing debt from operating cash flow, let alone absorb an additional $3,000,000 facility. The 12.0% EBITDA contraction, severe customer concentration, and 76.8% cash decline compound the risk. Proceeding would expose Pecos Capital Partners to a borrower unable to meet its obligations without asset sales or equity injection. The stated use of proceeds (refinancing seller notes) provides no improvement in debt service capacity. 

No structural mitigant—additional collateral, guarantees, or cash sweep—can offset the fundamental insufficiency of operating cash flow to support the requested facility size and structure.
